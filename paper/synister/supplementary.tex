\begin{abstract}
This Supplementary Information gives the formal specification and correctness
arguments for the Synister implementation in SynKit.  It separates exact
numeric-shell, globally minimal-shell and explicitly conditioned queries;
classifies exact-shell feasibility; proves the binary edit-support
decomposition, assignment bounds, verified-subgroup orbital branching,
certificate replay and implicit-hydrogen lifting; and records the validation
and evidence contracts.  The purpose is to retain mathematical detail without
expanding the journal-facing Article into the earlier 38-page development
manuscript.  Every production claim below corresponds to the final SynKit
source and its resource-bounded tests.  Historical Lewis-resource derivations
are retained separately and are not silently substituted for the heavy-atom
bond-order objective used in the reported FlowER pilot.
\end{abstract}

\section{Objects, objectives and query scopes}

\subsection{Endpoint graphs and compatible maps}

Let
\(
 R=(V_R,A,\chi_R)
\)
and
\(
 P=(V_P,B,\chi_P)
\)
be finite attributed endpoint graphs.  The matrices
\(A\in\R^{n\times n}\) and \(B\in\R^{n\times n}\) contain non-negative
bond weights.  The implementation accepts the directed matrix representation
and does not infer symmetry from an undirected view.  Molecular inputs give
symmetric matrices with zero diagonal.  The colour maps \(\chi_R,\chi_P\)
encode atomic number for assignment compatibility; declared unary properties
may additionally colour automorphism and structural-class computations.

\begin{definition}[Atom-compatible assignment space]
The global assignment space is
\[
 \Omega(R,P)=\left\{\pi:V_R\to V_P:
   \pi\text{ is bijective and }
   \chi_R(i)=\chi_P(\pi(i))\text{ for every }i\right\}.
\]
If element \(e\) occurs \(n_e\) times at each endpoint, then
\(|\Omega|=\prod_e n_e!\).  Unequal colour multiplicities make \(\Omega\)
empty.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Chemical distance]
For \(\pi\in\Omega\), let
\begin{equation}
 \CD_{R,P}(\pi)=\frac12\sum_{i,j\in V_R}
 \left|A_{ij}-B_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}\right|.
 \label{eq:si-cd}
\end{equation}
The factor one half counts each molecular bond once when the matrices are
symmetric.  It retains the exact matrix objective for general public graph
inputs.
\end{definition}

Binary mode replaces every non-zero edge weight by one before evaluating
\cref{eq:si-cd}.  Weighted mode retains bond order.  Self-loops are discarded
by the molecular graph adapter and are not part of the reported objective.

\subsection{Three non-interchangeable exact questions}

\begin{definition}[Global numeric shell]
For supplied \(C\geq0\),
\[
 \mathcal M_C(R,P)=\{\pi\in\Omega(R,P):\CD(\pi)=C\}.
\]
A complete result contains every selected mapping in its declared output
scope, or proves that this set is empty.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Global minimal shell]
Let \(C^*=\min_{\pi\in\Omega}\CD(\pi)\).  The minimal shell is
\(
 \mathcal M_*=\mathcal M_{C^*}
\).
Its exact computation has two logical obligations: prove \(C^*\), then close
the shell at that fixed value.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Conditioned shell]
For an injective compatible partial map \(F\), let
\[
 \Omega_F=\{\pi\in\Omega:\pi(i)=F(i)\text{ for every }i\in\supp(F)\}.
\]
A fixed-map or reference-core query searches \(\Omega_F\), not \(\Omega\).
Its completeness statement is therefore conditional and cannot be reported as
a global shell unless an independent proof establishes
\(\mathcal M_C\subseteq\Omega_F\).
\end{definition}

The final blinded FlowER experiment uses the first two global scopes.  A
reference-free SLAP result may provide a feasible incumbent and deterministic
branch order, but it does not remove a compatible assignment.  Reference-CD
mode reveals only the scalar \(C\); minimal mode reveals neither the reference
map nor its score until after search.

The application interface also permits a declared \emph{reference-assisted
global} execution.  The reference may define the numeric value (C), provide a
feasible incumbent, or order branches, but it is never converted into a fixed
assignment.  This remains a global-shell query.  A seed can improve or worsen
the traversal and therefore carries no unconditional runtime guarantee.

\subsection{Result-state contract}

The three terminal states have mathematical meaning.

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{complete}: at least one mapping was selected and the declared
  shell and scope are closed;
\item \texttt{no\_solutions}: the declared shell and scope are closed and
  empty;
\item \texttt{timeout}: a time, node, output or subordinate canonicalization
  bound left an unresolved frontier.
\end{itemize}

Only the first two states imply completeness.  A pre-search assignment-space
cap raises explicitly.  A mapping cap or callback interruption returns an
incomplete result rather than changing the requested mathematical set.

\section{Complexity boundary}

\subsection{Exact-shell and threshold feasibility}

\begin{theorem}[Exact-shell NP-completeness]
For simple vertex-coloured graphs and a supplied non-negative integer \(C\),
deciding whether \(\mathcal M_C(R,P)\) is non-empty is NP-complete.
\label{thm:si-exact-np}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The problem is in NP: a colour-preserving bijection is a polynomial-size
witness, and \cref{eq:si-cd} is evaluated in \(O(n^2)\) arithmetic operations.

For hardness, reduce from \textsc{Clique}.  Let \((G,k)\) be an instance with
\(n\) vertices and \(m\) edges, and write \(c=\binom{k}{2}\).  When \(m<c\),
the instance is necessarily negative and can be mapped to a fixed negative
exact-shell instance.  Otherwise take \(R=G\) and let \(P\) be the disjoint
union of \(K_k\) and \(n-k\) isolated vertices, with one common vertex colour.
Every bijection \(\pi\) chooses the \(k\)-vertex preimage \(S\) of the product
clique.  The binary symmetric difference is
\[
 \CD(\pi)=m+c-2|E(G[S])|.
\]
Because \(|E(G[S])|\leq c\), equality
\(\CD(\pi)=m-c\) holds exactly when \(G[S]\) is a \(k\)-clique.  Thus the
supplied shell \(C=m-c\) is non-empty exactly for positive Clique instances.
The transformation is polynomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Threshold and optimization complexity]
Deciding whether some compatible map has \(\CD\leq K\) is NP-complete, and
minimum-CD optimization is NP-hard.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The same construction has minimum at least \(m-c\), with equality exactly
when the Clique instance is positive.  Membership of the threshold problem in
NP is immediate.  A polynomial-time optimizer would decide the threshold
problem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Zero shell]
At \(C=0\), exact-shell feasibility is coloured graph isomorphism.  Graph
isomorphism is in NP but is not known to be NP-complete.  The reduction in
\cref{thm:si-exact-np} uses a supplied non-zero shell in general and does not
alter this special status.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Counting and output size}

\begin{proposition}[Counting hardness]
Counting the labeled maps in an exact binary shell is \(\#\mathrm P\)-hard
and belongs to \(\#\mathrm P\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the reduction above, every \(k\)-clique contributes exactly
\(k!(n-k)!\) bijections to shell \(m-c\): the clique and isolated product
vertices can each be permuted arbitrarily.  Dividing the shell count by this
known factor recovers the number of \(k\)-cliques.  Membership follows because
a nondeterministically guessed compatible bijection is checked in polynomial
time.
\end{proof}

If a shell contains \(L\) labeled mappings, explicitly writing length-\(n\)
arrays costs \(\Omega(Ln)\) time and output space.  The empty graphs on \(n\)
equally coloured vertices have \(n!\) zero-distance maps.  Consequently,
failure to finish a large shell is not by itself evidence of a poor
implementation: factorial output is part of the problem specification.
Compact subgroup representatives, counts and streamed digests avoid retaining
the output in memory, but they do not turn explicit enumeration into a
polynomial-output task.

\section{Exact binary edit-support decomposition}

Assume simple undirected binary graphs.  Let \(m_R=|E_R|\) and
\(m_P=|E_P|\).  For map \(\pi\), define
\[
 D_R(\pi)=E_R\setminus \pi^{-1}(E_P),\qquad
 D_P(\pi)=E_P\setminus \pi(E_R).
\]
These are the broken reactant edges and formed product edges.

\begin{lemma}[Edit budgets]
If \(\CD(\pi)=C\), then
\begin{equation}
 d_R:=|D_R|=\frac{C+m_R-m_P}{2},\qquad
 d_P:=|D_P|=\frac{C-m_R+m_P}{2}.
 \label{eq:si-edit-budget}
\end{equation}
Thus a shell is empty whenever these values are not non-negative integers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Binary CD is \(|D_R|+|D_P|\).  The conserved edge count gives
\(m_R-|D_R|=m_P-|D_P|\).  Solving the two linear equations yields
\cref{eq:si-edit-budget}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Support--isomorphism bijection]
For feasible \(d_R,d_P\), mappings in \(\mathcal M_C\) are in bijection with
triples \((S_R,S_P,\phi)\) satisfying
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(S_R\subseteq E_R\), \(|S_R|=d_R\), and
  \(S_P\subseteq E_P\), \(|S_P|=d_P\);
\item \(\phi:R-S_R\cong P-S_P\) is a colour-preserving spanning-graph
  isomorphism; and
\item rescoring \(\phi\) on the original endpoints gives mismatch sets
  exactly \(S_R,S_P\).
\end{enumerate}
\label{thm:si-edit-support}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(\pi\in\mathcal M_C\), choose
\(S_R=D_R(\pi)\) and \(S_P=D_P(\pi)\).  Every remaining reactant edge is
transported to a remaining product edge, and every remaining product edge has
a remaining preimage, so \(\pi\) is the required spanning-graph isomorphism.

Conversely, an isomorphism satisfying condition 3 has exactly \(d_R+d_P=C\)
mismatched edges and therefore lies in \(\mathcal M_C\).  Finally, the
mismatch sets are uniquely determined by a complete map.  The same map cannot
be accepted under a different support pair, so the correspondence is
injective as well as surjective.
\end{proof}

The implementation enumerates support pairs only up to a declared estimate,
buckets residual graphs by safe rejection invariants, enumerates exact
colour-preserving isomorphisms, and independently rescores every complete map.
Hash or invariant collisions can add work but cannot add an unverified map or
remove an isomorphism.  The automatic hybrid selector invokes this backend
only for a numeric, binary, labeled-output query without fixed assignments,
certificate generation or quotient output.  Otherwise it selects assignment
branch-and-bound.  The selector therefore changes computation, not scientific
scope.

\section{Assignment branch-and-bound}

\subsection{Prefix decomposition}

Fix a deterministic reactant order \(r_1,\ldots,r_n\).  At depth \(d\), a
prefix assigns \(r_1,\ldots,r_d\) to distinct compatible product vertices.
Let \(S\) and \(T\) be the assigned reactant and product sets, with remaining
sets \(U,V\).  The cost of any completion decomposes into
\[
 \CD(\pi)=C_{S,T}+C_{S,U;T,V}(\pi)+C_{U,V}(\pi),
\]
where the first term is fully committed, the second crosses the prefix cut,
and the third is internal to the residual sets.

For remaining compatible pair \((i,p)\), define its exact prefix interaction
\begin{equation}
 X_{ip}=\frac12\sum_{j\in S}
 \left(|A_{ij}-B_{p,\pi(j)}|+|A_{ji}-B_{\pi(j),p}|\right).
 \label{eq:si-cross}
\end{equation}
The implementation maintains all \(X_{ip}\) incrementally: assigning one
pair adds its contribution to each remaining pair; backtracking subtracts the
same contribution.  This uses dense \(O(n^2)\) working storage rather than an
\(O(n^4)\) pair tensor.

\subsection{Element-block assignment interval}

Let \(\Phi(U,V)\) be the compatible bijections between remaining vertices.
Because colour classes do not interact in the all-different constraint, a
linear assignment can be solved separately inside each element block.

\begin{lemma}[Cross-cut assignment bounds]
Let
\[
 L_X=\min_{\phi\in\Phi(U,V)}\sum_{i\in U}X_{i,\phi(i)},\qquad
 U_X=\max_{\phi\in\Phi(U,V)}\sum_{i\in U}X_{i,\phi(i)}.
\]
For every completion \(\pi\),
\(L_X\leq C_{S,U;T,V}(\pi)\leq U_X\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:si-cross} counts exactly all assigned--unassigned matrix
entries for the chosen residual bijection.  The chosen completion is feasible
for the two extrema, so its value lies between them.  Independent Hungarian
solutions by colour block give the same extrema as a full matrix with
infinite incompatible entries.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Residual bond-mass interval}

For the residual submatrices write
\[
 a_U=\sum_{i,j\in U}A_{ij},\quad
 b_V=\sum_{p,q\in V}B_{pq},\quad
 \alpha_U=\sum_{i,j\in U}|A_{ij}|,\quad
 \beta_V=\sum_{p,q\in V}|B_{pq}|.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Internal interval]
For every residual completion,
\begin{equation}
 \frac12|a_U-b_V|
 \leq C_{U,V}(\pi)
 \leq \frac12(\alpha_U+\beta_V).
 \label{eq:si-mass}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Permutation preserves the sum of entries of \(B_V\).  The lower bound is the
reverse triangle inequality
\(
 \sum|x_k-y_k|\geq|\sum x_k-\sum y_k|
\).
The upper bound follows from
\(|x_k-y_k|\leq|x_k|+|y_k|\).  Multiplication by one half matches
\cref{eq:si-cd}.
\end{proof}

Combining the preceding lemmas gives the subtree interval
\begin{equation}
 C_{S,T}+L_X+\frac12|a_U-b_V|
 \leq \CD(\pi)\leq
 C_{S,T}+U_X+\frac12(\alpha_U+\beta_V).
 \label{eq:si-subtree}
\end{equation}
A numeric target below the lower endpoint or above the upper endpoint cannot
occur in that subtree.  Minimization needs only the lower endpoint against a
verified feasible incumbent.

\subsection{Incident-profile pair rejection}

For atom \(i\), partition incident bond weights by neighbour colour and sort
within every block.  Do the same for candidate image \(p\).  Pairing the
sorted one-dimensional lists minimizes an \(L^1\) matching cost.  Padding by
zeros accounts for different degrees.

\begin{lemma}[Profile admissibility]
Half the sum of the per-colour sorted-list \(L^1\) differences is a lower bound
on the total CD contribution of pairs incident to \(i\) under any completion
with \(\pi(i)=p\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every compatible completion pairs neighbours only with vertices of the same
colour block.  For one-dimensional absolute difference, monotone pairing is
optimal by the exchange argument: replacing crossing pairs
\((x_a,y_b),(x_b,y_a)\) with ordered pairs cannot increase cost.  Dropping
global consistency between the independently optimized blocks is a
relaxation, hence a lower bound.  The factor one half prevents double counting
the directed matrix entries.
\end{proof}

Pairs whose profile lower bound alone exceeds a numeric target may be removed.
The rule is not used to claim an upper bound or to impose an arbitrary order
inside a molecular orbit.

\subsection{Search completeness and two-pass streaming}

\begin{theorem}[Assignment enumerator correctness]
Suppose arithmetic comparisons use the declared tolerance, every compatible
child not excluded by a proved bound is visited, and no resource cap fires.
Then numeric mode returns exactly \(\mathcal M_C\).  Minimal two-pass mode
returns exactly \(\mathcal M_*\) and its reported minimum is global.
\label{thm:si-bnb}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Without pruning, depth-first traversal visits every compatible bijection once.
Committed-cost, profile and interval pruning remove only subtrees whose every
completion lies outside the target or above the incumbent, by the preceding
lemmas.  Numeric leaves are independently evaluated and retained exactly when
their cost equals \(C\).

For minimal mode, the first pass begins from any verified feasible incumbent
and can replace it only by a strictly lower complete-map cost.  Lower-bound
pruning cannot remove a completion below the incumbent.  Exhaustion therefore
proves \(C^*\).  The second pass is the already-correct numeric traversal at
\(C^*\).  Callbacks are invoked only in this second pass, so a provisional
optimizer is never streamed as final output.
\end{proof}

The seed affects only candidate and reactant ordering.  Erasing it yields the
same set with a different traversal.  A zero-time deadline, mapping cap or
interrupted callback leaves a non-empty logical frontier and cannot satisfy
the theorem's no-cap hypothesis.

\section{Verified symmetry and stabilizer branching}

\subsection{Exact witness requirement}

Let \(h\) be a product permutation.  Synister accepts it as a witness only if
\[
 \chi_P(h(p))=\chi_P(p)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 B_{h(p),h(q)}=B_{p,q}
 \quad\text{for every }p,q.
\]
This is checked against the exact directed objective matrix, not an
undirected visualization and not with the user-supplied CD tolerance.  A cache
entry is bound to a digest of the same graph data.  Hence every retained
generator lies in a concrete subgroup \(H\leq\Aut(P)\).  Incomplete discovery
may make \(H\) smaller than \(\Aut(P)\), but cannot introduce an invalid
generator.

\begin{lemma}[Cost invariance]
For \(h\in H\) and \(\pi\in\Omega\),
\(\CD(h\circ\pi)=\CD(\pi)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Product automorphism gives
\(B_{h(\pi(i)),h(\pi(j))}=B_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}\) for every ordered pair, so every
summand of \cref{eq:si-cd} is unchanged.  Colour preservation keeps
\(h\circ\pi\) inside \(\Omega\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Point stabilizers}

For selected prefix images \(y_1,\ldots,y_{d-1}\), define
\[
 H_{d-1}=\Stab_H(y_1,\ldots,y_{d-1})
 =\{h\in H:h(y_j)=y_j\text{ for }j<d\}.
\]
The legal next-image domain is partitioned into \(H_{d-1}\)-orbits.  Orbital
branching keeps one deterministic representative from each orbit and updates
the point stabilizer after the choice.

\begin{theorem}[One representative per verified-subgroup orbit]
If the stabilizer chain used at every visited prefix is complete for the
verified subgroup \(H\), orbital branching visits exactly one leaf from every
\(H\)-orbit on \(\Omega\).
\label{thm:si-orbital}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For existence, take any leaf \(\pi\).  If its first image is not the retained
representative of its \(H\)-orbit, compose with an element transporting that
image to the representative.  At depth two, use an element of the stabilizer
of the first retained image to transport the second choice, and continue.
Composition produces an equivalent leaf whose every prefix choice is
retained.

For uniqueness, suppose two retained leaves \(\pi,\rho\) are related by
\(h\in H\).  Let \(d\) be their first differing position.  Their earlier
images agree, so \(h\in H_{d-1}\).  Their two depth-\(d\) images therefore lie
in the same \(H_{d-1}\)-orbit, contradicting retention of only one candidate
from that orbit.
\end{proof}

This proof explains why merely sorting all atoms in a global orbit is invalid:
an element transporting the next candidate must fix the already selected
prefix.  Orbit transitivity without the point-stabilizer condition does not
justify independent exchanges.  The relabelled \(C_5\) regression in the test
suite detects precisely this failure mode.

\begin{proposition}[Free action and labeled expansion]
The product-side action of \(H\) on complete bijections is free.  Consequently
every orbit has size \(|H|\), and expanding each unique representative by all
elements of \(H\) recovers every labeled mapping exactly once.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(h\circ\pi=\pi\), surjectivity of \(\pi\) implies that \(h\) fixes every
product vertex; hence \(h\) is the identity.  Freeness makes every stabilizer
of a complete mapping trivial, so orbit--stabilizer gives orbit size \(|H|\).
Distinct representative orbits are disjoint.
\end{proof}

The production implementation uses bounded Schreier-style stabilizer
operations.  If a work limit prevents completion of a deeper stabilizer,
symmetry reduction is disabled below that point and quotient-completeness
metadata is false.  Full labeled mode can always run without quotienting.  An
alternative expansion mode deliberately restricts to a fully enumerated
verified cyclic subgroup, making duplicate-free expansion auditable without
materializing an unknown full group.

\section{Replayable prefix-cover certificates}

\subsection{Tree-cover representation}

The compatible assignment tree has one root-to-leaf path per mapping.  A
certificate binds the input matrices, colours, binary policy, reactant order,
target, tolerance and selected mapping digest.  Disjoint prefixes are marked
as one of:

\begin{itemize}
\item committed-cost terminal pruning;
\item assignment lower-bound pruning;
\item numeric assignment upper-bound pruning;
\item verified symmetry pruning with a concrete transporter;
\item unresolved timeout frontier; or
\item complete leaf evaluation.
\end{itemize}

The verifier constructs a trie, rejects overlapping prefixes, and requires the
children of every unmarked internal node to be exactly its remaining
atom-compatible images.

\begin{theorem}[Certificate soundness]
If independent replay accepts a certificate with no frontier, the reported
status, shell count, selected mapping digest and declared quotient scope follow
from the bound and symmetry conditions encoded in the certificate.
\label{thm:si-certificate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Input and certificate SHA-256 bindings first prevent replay against different
endpoint data or unsigned metadata.  Prefix-freeness makes marked subtrees
disjoint.  Exact child equality at every unmarked node proves, inductively,
that their union covers the compatible assignment tree.

For each cost marker, replay recomputes the committed cost and the applicable
bound from the bound matrices rather than trusting search counters.  By
\cref{eq:si-subtree}, the marked subtree contains no selected leaf.  For a
symmetry marker, replay checks the full product permutation exactly and checks
that it transports the rejected prefix to a lexicographically earlier prefix;
cost invariance then makes rejection safe in the declared quotient scope.
Every remaining complete leaf is rescored, and the sorted mapping digest and
count are reconstructed.  With no frontier, every tree path is therefore
either safely rejected or evaluated exactly once.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fail-closed interruption]
A certificate containing any frontier prefix cannot prove shell completeness.
\end{corollary}

The current prefix-cover format deliberately requires collected mappings,
because its digest binds their complete sorted set.  Streaming and count-only
queries remain exact computations but do not claim this particular
certificate type.  This separation avoids calling a partial or memory-bounded
transcript a proof it does not encode.

\section{Exact reaction-centre and structural spectra}

\subsection{Mapping-dependent changes}

For mapping \(\pi\), define the ordered bond change
\[
 \Delta^\pi_{ij}=B_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}-A_{ij}.
\]
Changed bonds have non-zero \(\Delta^\pi_{ij}\); changed atoms include their
endpoints and atoms whose declared transported unary properties differ.
For a complete selected shell \(\mathcal M\), define
\[
 I=\bigcap_{\pi\in\mathcal M}\supp(\Delta^\pi),\qquad
 U=\bigcup_{\pi\in\mathcal M}\supp(\Delta^\pi).
\]
The intersection \(I\) is the invariant centre, while \(U\setminus I\) is the
mapping-dependent region.  Exact counters give each atom or bond's change
frequency.  A deterministic stream digest binds the observed representative
sequence.

\subsection{ITS and boundary-aware template identity}

An ITS graph places before/after attributes on a common transported vertex
set.  Vertex colours include atomic number and declared before/after unary
properties; edge colours include before/after bond orders.  SynKit's exact
coloured-graph canonicalizer produces an injective serialization of the
canonical certificate.  Hashes identify serialized codes but are never used
as an equality oracle.

A radius-\(r\) template contains the changed atoms and their radius-\(r\)
neighbourhood in the union graph.  Every edge crossing the retained boundary
is encoded at its retained endpoint by the outside element and before/after
orders.  This prevents removal of external context from merging templates
whose chemical resource boundary differs.

\begin{proposition}[Structural invariance on product orbits]
If \(\rho=h\circ\pi\) for \(h\in H\leq\Aut(P)\), the ITS graphs induced by
\(\pi\) and \(\rho\) are colour-preserving isomorphic.  The same holds for
their boundary-aware templates.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the product automorphism \(h\) to relabel the transported product side.
It preserves product colours and every matrix entry.  Reactant attributes are
unchanged, so all paired before/after vertex and edge colours correspond.  The
union graph, graph distance from the changed set and every crossing-boundary
descriptor are consequently preserved.
\end{proof}

Thus one complete representative per verified subgroup orbit preserves the
set of exact ITS and template classes.  Because the product action is free and
all mapping orbits have the common size \(|H|\), normalized class frequencies
also agree between subgroup representatives and full labeled expansion.  The
reported mapping Hartley entropy is \(\log Q\) for \(Q\) selected verified-
subgroup representatives; structural Hartley entropy is \(\log K\) for
\(K\) exact ITS classes.  These are different objects and are stored in
different schema fields.

If canonicalization reaches a time or node bound, mapping-shell completeness
is unchanged but structural analysis is incomplete.  Such a record cannot
enter a denominator for exact class richness.

\subsection{Candidate-complete alternative-ITS panels}

Let \(\kappa(\pi)\) be the exact canonical ITS code of mapping \(\pi\), and let
\(\pi_0\) be a supplied reference.  For a numeric or proven-minimal shell
\(\mathcal M\), define the reference-relative class set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A(\mathcal M,\pi_0)
 =\{\kappa(\pi):\pi\in\mathcal M\}\setminus\{\kappa(\pi_0)\}.
 \label{eq:si-alternative-panel}
\end{equation}
The implementation retains a deterministic representative AAM for every
observed member of \(\mathcal A\), together with its representative-scope count
and, when the verified subgroup order is known, its labeled count.

\begin{proposition}[Alternative-panel completeness]
If the global shell search closes and every requested canonicalization closes,
the reported alternative identifiers equal
\(\mathcal A(\mathcal M,\pi_0)\).  This remains true under safe verified-product
symmetry pruning even when the verified subgroup is a proper subgroup of
\(\Aut(P)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Without symmetry pruning, every \(\pi\in\mathcal M\) reaches the accumulator.
With pruning, each removed prefix has a verified transporter to a retained
prefix under a product automorphism that preserves every unary attribute used
by the ITS code.  By structural invariance, the removed and retained leaves
have the same \(\kappa\).  Incomplete automorphism discovery can retain extra
representatives but cannot delete a class.  Exact canonical codes then group
precisely the isomorphism classes, and removing \(\kappa(\pi_0)\) gives
\cref{eq:si-alternative-panel}.  A mapping or canonicalization frontier makes
the application result incomplete instead of invoking this conclusion.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{negative} is appropriate only relative to a modeling task in
which the supplied ITS is the positive label.  Membership in \(\mathcal A\)
does not establish energetic infeasibility, mechanistic impossibility or an
annotation error; it establishes an exact structural alternative under the
declared endpoint and CD policy.

\section{Exact implicit-hydrogen lifting}

Fix a heavy-atom map and transport product hydrogen counts into reactant
order.  Let \(r_i,p_i\in\N\) be the implicit hydrogen counts on heavy parent
\(i\) before and after transport.  The implementation requires
\(\sum_i r_i=\sum_i p_i\); unequal inventories are rejected.

\begin{definition}[Hydrogen flow]
A non-negative integer matrix \(X=(x_{ij})\) is feasible when
\[
 \sum_jx_{ij}=r_i,\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}=p_j.
\]
Cell \(x_{ij}\) counts hydrogens whose reactant parent is \(i\) and product
parent is \(j\).  The diagonal is preserved parentage and off-diagonal cells
are transfers.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Minimum transfer plans]
The minimum number of broken hydrogen--parent bonds, equivalently formed
bonds, is
\[
 T=\sum_i(r_i-p_i)_+=\sum_i(p_i-r_i)_+.
\]
Every minimum plan has
\(x_{ii}=\min(r_i,p_i)\) and an arbitrary non-negative donor--acceptor flow
with the residual row and column sums.  Enumerating all such flows enumerates
all chemically distinct minimum parent-transfer plans.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At most \(\min(r_i,p_i)\) hydrogens can keep parent \(i\), giving an upper
bound \(\sum_i\min(r_i,p_i)\) on preserved bonds.  Taking every diagonal cell
at this value leaves surplus only at donors and deficit only at acceptors.
Balanced totals guarantee a feasible transportation flow.  It preserves the
upper-bound number of bonds and therefore is minimum.  Conversely, any plan
with a smaller diagonal entry at one parent sacrifices a preservable bond and
cannot be minimum.  Residual flows are in one-to-one correspondence with the
remaining minimum plans.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Labeled hydrogen multiplicity]
For feasible cell counts \(X\), the number of explicit labeled-hydrogen
bijections realizing the flow is
\begin{equation}
 N(X)=\frac{\prod_i r_i!\prod_jp_j!}{\prod_{i,j}x_{ij}!}.
 \label{eq:si-hcount}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each reactant parent \(i\), partition its \(r_i\) labeled hydrogens among
destination cells in
\(r_i!/\prod_jx_{ij}!\) ways.  At each product parent \(j\), biject the
selected incoming hydrogens with its \(p_j\) labeled product hydrogens in
\(p_j!\) ways.  Multiplication over all rows and columns yields
\cref{eq:si-hcount}.
\end{proof}

The algorithm sums \(N(X)\) without constructing explicit hydrogen vertices.
Exhaustive controls through four labeled hydrogens on three parents compare
this value and every plan against literal hydrogen-bijection enumeration.  A
plan cap returns \texttt{complete=False}; it does not reinterpret the partial
sum as the total multiplicity.

\section{Reference blinding and evidence integrity}

\subsection{Independent endpoint blinding}

Mapped input is used to construct a held-out correspondence and scalar
reference CD.  Reactant and product atoms are then independently permuted with
different deterministic salts.  Map labels are stripped from the search
graphs.  Explicit hydrogens are folded into per-parent counts for the reported
heavy-atom experiment.  Atomic symbols are normalized to integer atomic
numbers before SLAP or exact matching, preventing accidental string/integer
colour mismatch.

The reference-free SLAP seed is computed only from the blinded endpoints.  It
is repaired to a complete compatible bijection by graph matching and
assignment completion, then independently evaluated before becoming an
incumbent.  The held-out map is passed only to post-search tests of exact map
or exact canonical ITS-class inclusion.

\subsection{Immutable record contract}

The serial runner uses one process and one numerical thread.  Before work it
sets a hard address-space limit and records every search and structure bound.
Each gzip JSON case contains the dataset row identity, blinded-order digests,
options, result scopes, completion flags and a canonical payload digest.  The
manifest binds dataset, source implementation, schema and options.  Resume
accepts a case only if its payload, reaction and manifest bindings all match.
Writes use a temporary file followed by atomic replacement.

The manuscript summarizer rechecks every case digest and the manifest digest
before deriving statistics.  A case contributes only when enumeration and the
specific downstream computation are complete.  The one-second pilot is
therefore a resource-censored validation cohort, not a random or representative
estimate of the FlowER population.

\section{Implementation invariants and resource bounds}

\subsection{Output, working memory and certificate memory}

The assignment tree has depth \(n\).  Its dense endpoint matrices and
incremental cross-cost matrix require \(O(n^2)\) storage.  The current mapping,
used-image vector, residual block indices and depth-first stack require
\(O(n)\) additional storage.  Verified strong generators and their bounded
stabilizer data add an explicitly limited group term.  Thus a count-only or
callback traversal need not retain memory proportional to the number of
selected mappings.

Collected labeled output necessarily uses \(\Theta(Ln)\) integers for \(L\)
mappings.  The present replayable prefix-cover certificate stores terminal,
bound, symmetry and frontier prefixes and can likewise scale with the explored
tree.  It is therefore deliberately incompatible with count-only streaming.
This is an API guarantee rather than a hidden optimization: users choose
bounded collection, uncollected streaming, or a collected replay certificate.
No mode claims all three at arbitrary output size.

The edit-support backend stores only the support pair currently being tested,
residual graphs and its bounded selected output.  Its combinatorial work
estimate is
\[
 \binom{m_R}{d_R}\binom{m_P}{d_P}.
\]
This estimate controls backend selection; it is not presented as a fixed-
parameter tractability result.  In the worst case both the number of support
pairs and the number of isomorphisms are exponential.

\subsection{Metadata separates proof obligations}

The result record distinguishes target, selected cost, independently proved
global minimum, raw compatible-bijection count, selected representative count,
selected labeled count, mapping scope, subgroup order, quotient completeness,
backend decision, search status and truncation reason.  These fields prevent
three common category errors: interpreting the lowest partial cost as a proved
minimum; interpreting subgroup representatives as all labeled maps; and
interpreting a timeout with a found reference as a complete reference shell.

For a numeric query, users may disable the separate optimization pass.  The
requested shell can still be complete, but \texttt{minimum\_cost} is then
unset rather than filled by the lowest selected or visited mapping.  For a
minimal query, two-pass optimization and enumeration remain inseparable.
Backend metadata records whether assignment or edit support was used and why;
the output schema and scope are otherwise identical.

\section{Conclusion}

The formal result is deliberately set-valued.  For a declared global shell,
Synister either returns every mapping in the declared labeled or verified-
subgroup scope, proves the shell empty, or exposes unresolved work.  Binary
edit support and assignment branch-and-bound are independent exact routes;
verified stabilizers compress only proved symmetries; certificates replay a
complete decision-tree cover; and hydrogen flow restores implicit-H
multiplicity after heavy-atom search.  NP-completeness and factorial output
preclude a universal fast algorithm, but they do not preclude rigorous,
resource-aware closure on chemically relevant instances.  The frozen pilot
uses that distinction to show that endpoint-optimal atom mapping can be
structurally non-identifiable even when the score is exact, and the application
record turns that ambiguity into candidate-complete reference-relative AAM
panels without calling structural alternatives mechanistic errors.
